# Citizenship test reel scripts

Source articles: `src/data/blog-posts.ts` (citizenship-test batch, Nov 2026).

Every script opens with a one-line cold frame (what the test is) before the
specific angle, because each reel needs to work as a stranger's first
touchpoint — not just for people who already saw an earlier one.

**The CTA tag itself never changes** — it's the same 5-second pre-recorded
clip every time:

> "I'm making a tool that helps you study for this. Join the waitlist at ___ FinlandCitizenshipTest.fi."

What varies is *where* you cut to it. Two patterns, marked per script:

- **[END-CTA]** — finish the point, hard cut to the tag, done. Use this when
  the script is dense and there's no natural pause to interrupt.
- **[MID-CTA]** — stop mid-script with a short bridge line, cut to the tag,
  then cut back with a short re-entry line and finish the point. This holds
  people past the "ad" because they want the rest of the content. Don't
  overuse it — roughly half the scripts below use it, half end-cut, so it
  doesn't become its own predictable pattern.

The bridge-in and re-entry lines are written out below so you're not
improvising the transition on camera.

## 1. The master overview — [END-CTA]
*(source: finnish-citizenship-test)*

Finland is bringing in a new citizenship test, starting March 1st, 2027. If you're applying for Finnish citizenship, you now have to pass it first. It's called the kansalaisuuskoe.

Here's what we know: it's on a computer, on site, multiple choice. Only in Finnish or Swedish — no English. There'll be 6 to 10 test days a year, in seven cities: Helsinki, Tampere, Turku, Oulu, Kuopio, Vaasa, Rovaniemi. There's a fee — amount not announced yet. And once you pass, it never expires.

It covers laws, your rights, equality, and Finnish history and culture — all written by the University of Helsinki.

*(hard cut to CTA tag)*

*(source: finnish-citizenship-test-reading-test)*

It's kind of a reading test...
Starting 2027, Finland requires a new citizenship test for most adults applying for citizenship. Nobody's talking about this part: it's officially about civics, but it's only offered in Finnish or Swedish. Which makes it secretly a Finnish reading test.

**Bridge in:** "Before I show you the loophole it closes — this is exactly the kind of thing I'm building a study tool for. Join the waitlist, link's below."

**Re-entry:** "Okay, here's the loophole —"

For citizenship, YKI only requires two of: speaking, writing, listening, reading. Two out of three accepted combos don't even include reading. So you can meet the language requirement for citizenship without ever proving you can read Finnish well. This test changes that. If your reading is weak, this is where it shows.

What level of Finnish do you need for the new citizenship exam? 
Finland's new citizenship test, starting 2027, doesn't officially set a Finnish level. But here's what level you actually need.

The language requirement for citizenship is YKI 3 — that's B1. My honest take: if your reading is around B1, you can pass — but only if you study the real material in Finnish. Reading it once in English and hoping for the best won't work, because the questions won't be in English.

If you're below that, expect long compound words, the passive voice everywhere, and multiple-choice traps where one word flips the whole meaning.

## 6. Vocabulary round 2 — rights & equality — [END-CTA]
*(source: finnish-citizenship-test-vocabulary)*

Finland's new citizenship test, starting 2027, covers your fundamental rights — in Finnish. So learn these first.

Perusoikeudet — fundamental rights. Sananvapaus — freedom of speech. Yhdenvertaisuus — equal treatment for everyone. Tasa-arvo — equality, usually about gender.

Here's a trick: tasa-arvo and yhdenvertaisuus both mean "equality" in English — but they're not the same word, and a multiple-choice question will absolutely test the difference.

## 10. The biggest study mistake — [END-CTA]
*(source: how-to-study-for-finnish-citizenship-test)*

Finland's new citizenship test, starting 2027, is in Finnish — and the biggest mistake people will make studying for it is starting with a translation instead.

If you read the English version first, your brain relaxes — and the test is in Finnish. You need to recognize "perusoikeudet" on the screen, not "fundamental rights."

Also: watch for small words that flip a sentence. Ei, vain, aina, kaikki — not, only, always, all. And watch case endings — eduskunnassa versus eduskunnalle can be the difference between two answers.

*(hard cut to CTA tag)*

## 12. Who's exempt — degrees & health — [END-CTA]
*(source: who-is-exempt-from-finnish-citizenship-test)*

Finland's introducing a new citizenship test in 2027 for most people applying for citizenship. Here's a loophole most people don't know about.

If you've completed a Finnish- or Swedish-language degree in Finland — bachelor's, master's, even a doctorate — you skip the citizenship test entirely. Same if you did your matriculation exam in Finnish or Swedish.

But it has to be the language of the degree, not just where you studied. An English-language degree from a Finnish university does not exempt you.

There's also a health and disability exemption — but it needs documentation, and it has to be long-term.
